# AI Contribution Policy

This project does not police how you write your contributions, including whether you use AI tools. It does, however, need every contribution to be backed by a person who owns it, both technically and legally.

The copyright status of AI-generated work is still uncertain, and AI tools can reproduce code from their training data. Accepting contributions without a clear human owner could put the project's license at risk.

## By submitting a contribution, you confirm that

- You are the author of the contribution, or have the legal right to submit it.
- You can license it under the project's license, and it does not include code copied from sources with an incompatible license.
- You understand every part of it and take full responsibility for it.

"An AI wrote it" is not an acceptable answer to a question or a review comment. If you cannot explain and defend a change, do not submit it. Open an issue describing the problem instead.

## AI co-authors

AI tools cannot hold copyright, so they cannot be listed as authors. Commits and pull requests with an AI tool in a `Co-authored-by:` trailer, or any other author credit, will not be merged. Remove those credits and take ownership of the change before submitting.

## Autonomous agents

Contributions from autonomous agents, bots, or automated accounts are not accepted, and accounts that behave like bots, such as opening pull requests in bulk, may be banned.

## Summary

- Your tooling is your choice
- Every contribution must have a human author who can license it
- AI tools must not be credited as co-authors
- Autonomous agents and bots are not allowed
- Contribution guidelines must still be followed

Read more about this and other AI contribution policies on [ai-policy.dev](https://ai-policy.dev/)
